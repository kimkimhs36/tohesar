Small cars.
Big feelings.

The garage in your head doesn’t need to make sense.

Editorial interpretation

Some cars stay with us long after the road has disappeared. Shrink them down, take away the showroom, and what remains is surprisingly personal.

A collection is a way of paying attention. Here, six very different cars share the same page: a Ferrari, two British grand tourers, an Audi, a Kia and a Chevrolet. They are a small selection from the candidates supplied for TOHEŞAR’s personal design collection—not a ranking, and not a reconstruction of the complete list.

Remembering a shape.

Think of the Ferrari 360 Modena without thinking of its badge. The rounded nose. The cabin drawn forward into the body. The dark opening behind the door. In miniature, those relationships become the whole story.

The Jaguar XK has a different rhythm: a long bonnet and a roof that slips gently into the tail. Beside it, the Aston Martin DBS feels more taut. These are visual readings of proportion, rather than claims about which car is better.

Not a dream garage.
A way of seeing.

An unlikely garage.

The first-generation Audi R8 breaks the flow with its contrasting sideblade. It is a strong graphic interruption—a dark surface that makes the cabin and the rear body read as separate pieces. A is as useful to an illustrator as it is to a designer: one gesture makes the car recognisable.

The Kia Stinger and second-generation Chevrolet Volt bring the composition back to everyday life. Their presence matters. An interesting collection can contain the spectacular and the familiar, a low sports car and a car you might see outside a supermarket. Affection does not have to follow a price list.

Arranging these cars like fragments on a page draws on the exuberance of turn-of-the-millennium graphic culture: airbrushed highlights, scattered motifs and the visual pleasure of collecting. It is a contemporary interpretation, not a claim that every vehicle belongs to that period.

A little world, made carefully.

The six illustrations were generated for this production study in a unified airbrush style, then presented as separate interactive layers. Only selected cars respond to touch; there is no continuous animation competing with the reading.

These are AI-assisted interpretations, not technical reference drawings. The Ferrari nose, the Jaguar lamps, wheel designs and other model-specific details still need a specialist illustration pass. No manufacturer endorsement is implied.
